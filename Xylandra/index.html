HOW TO USE THIS COMPANION
STARTING OUT
Go to Character first — fill in your traits or hit the roll buttons to generate a concept, skills, frailty, gear and name. Then go to Adventure Hook to generate your opening situation. It gives you a who/what/why/where seed dressed in Xylandra flavour with a short narrative summary you can log to the journal.
DURING A SCENE
Scene Engine is your home base. Fill in location, who's present, and your objective — the display at the top updates live. When you need to ask the oracle something, flip to Oracle, type your question and hit Consult. The advantage/disadvantage dropdown adjusts how many dice are rolled. Doubles automatically tick the Twist Counter — at 3 it fires a twist automatically.
WHEN YOU'RE STUCK
Inspiration is the aphantasia-friendly panel. Sensory Anchor gives you concrete sound, touch, smell and ground details rather than "picture this place." Fiction Prompts give you behavioural descriptions for NPCs, objects and places. Encounter Spark drops a random Xylandra situation into your lap when nothing is happening.
CONFLICT
Go to Conflict — set your opponent's name and starting Luck before you begin, then alternate Player Attacks and Opponent Attacks. Luck tracks update automatically. When someone hits zero they're in mortal danger — the next attack ends it. Context determines outcome: captured, injured, fled, or dead.
KEEPING RECORDS
Journal is for your own narrative writing and scene summaries. Play Log auto-fills itself from every oracle roll, encounter spark and conflict result — you don't need to maintain it, it maintains itself.
SAVING & PORTABILITY
localStorage saves automatically in your browser. Use the Export button at the end of each session to download a JSON backup. Use Import to reload it later or carry it to another device. The app works on any browser — just note that save data doesn't sync between devices automatically, export/import is your bridge.
ORACLE RESOLUTION
Roll 1 Chance Die (green) + 1 Risk Die (red).
Chance highest → YES  |  Risk highest → NO

Both low (1-3): add BUT
Both high (4-6): add AND

ADVANTAGE: roll 2 Chance + 1 Risk, keep highest Chance
DISADVANTAGE: roll 1 Chance + 2 Risk, keep highest Risk
TWIST COUNTER
Starts at 0. Every time both dice show the same number (doubles), add 1.
Counter below 3: treat result as Yes, But…
Counter reaches 3: a TWIST happens — roll on the Twist Table and reset to 0.
HARM & LUCK
All characters start with Luck 6.
In conflict, alternate turns. When you fail defence, take Harm. When you succeed, cause Harm.
When Luck reaches 0: face mortal danger — next attack ends the conflict.
Context determines outcome: captured, injured, fled, or dead.
CHARACTER TRAITS
Concept — adjective-noun pair describing the character
Skills (x2) — specific abilities, not common traits
Frailty — physical, mental, or social weakness
Gear (x2) — notable equipment (not everyday items)
Goal — long-term objective
Motive — what drives the goal
Nemesis — person or org that hinders the protagonist

At story end: add 1 new trait related to how it ended.
XYLANDRA SPECIAL RULES
Survival in Harsh Lands — Ask oracle for desert/jungle/sky travel. Yes+And = safe passage and bonus. No+And = lost, injured, or worse.

Relic Activation — Oracle determines if ancient tech functions. Failure may trigger guardian, alarm, or temporal anomaly.

Time Anomaly — Passing through fractured zones: Yes+And = glimpse future. No+And = displaced from time, memories lost.

Skyship Combat — Uses same Harm/Luck system. Ships have Luck traits. Frailties apply as disadvantages.
✦ LORE CODEX — XYLANDRA THE LOST PLANET
XYLANDRA — THE LOST PLANET
Xylandra is a savage world of ancient alien ruins, exotic civilizations, planetary warlords, airships, lost technologies, and primal conflict. Once a thriving nexus of cosmic power, it is now a fractured world, torn apart by forgotten wars, temporal distortions, and the struggle between its many species. Warriors carve out empires, explorers seek forbidden knowledge, and adventurers fight for survival among the wreckage of a fallen age.
A WORLD OUT OF TIME
Xylandra is lost in a temporal anomaly, drifting between past, present, and unknown futures. Some civilizations have existed for millennia; others arrived unexpectedly, pulled from distant worlds or eras. The sky holds fragments of a shattered moon, drifting in unstable orbits. Ancient ruins pulse with forgotten power, some activating at unpredictable intervals.
THE ETERNAL STRUGGLE
Warlords, merchant-kings, and rogue warbands fight for control over scarce resources and forgotten relics. Nomadic tribes navigate the vast deserts, jungles, and sky-reefs, surviving on trade and scavenging. Some factions seek to unlock the secrets of the Builders; others simply want power over those who already have it.
A HARSH, EXOTIC LANDSCAPE
Xylandra's environments range from endless dunes to bioluminescent jungles, volcanic wastelands, and towering crystalline spires. Ancient cities, partially reclaimed by nature, hold automated defenses, mechanical guardians, and lost knowledge. Some regions phase in and out of existence, revealing entire lost civilizations overnight. Unstable gravity wells, drifting landmasses, and skyborne ruins make navigation a perilous art.
WHAT IS XYLANDRA?
The planet's true nature is debated. Some believe it is a prison world — a place where civilizations are sent to be forgotten. Others claim it is a test, designed by the Builders to find worthy successors. A few insist it is a tomb, and that whatever the Builders were afraid of is still buried somewhere beneath the sands. Some say it is an experiment gone awry — and that the experiment is still running.
Xylandra's true history is fragmented, hidden within ancient ruins, oral traditions, and forbidden archives. Different cultures interpret events differently, leading to contradictory myths and competing truths. No single account is considered authoritative.
??? — THE ERA OF THE BUILDERS (MYTHIC TIME)
A great and advanced civilization ruled Xylandra. They constructed massive cities, created bioengineered life, and harnessed the planet's energies in ways no current civilization fully understands. Their machines and temples still function — automated, patient, waiting.

Something happened. A war. A rebellion. A cosmic disaster. No record survives that clearly explains their disappearance. Only their ruins remain, and the ruins do not give up their secrets easily.
UNKNOWN — THE FRACTURING
The sky split, and time itself became unstable. New species and civilizations appeared — some from other worlds, others from Xylandra's own past and future, dragged through temporal anomalies. The planet became a battleground, with warring factions trying to claim its ruins. No one knows how long this period lasted. Time was unreliable.
RECENT PAST — THE WARLORD AGE
The old empires collapsed, and new powers rose from the ashes. Sky-pirates, raiders, and warlords fought over the planet's few remaining stable territories. Some rediscovered forgotten technologies, while others turned to brute force and conquest. Factions formed, alliances broke, and the cycle of war became the only constant.
NOW — THE AGE OF SAVAGE BLADES
This is the era you play in. The Bone-Kings consolidate power in the wastes. The Skyborne Freeholds resist from above. The Rift Scholars dig too deep into things that shouldn't be touched. The Cult of the Builder-Gods guards ruins from those who would use them. The Ironfang Guild sells its blade to whoever pays. And somewhere in the Riftlands, the Ghosts of the Fracture drift between moments, belonging to no time at all.

Your story begins here.
THE BONE-KINGS OF THE WASTES
Concept: Warlord Tyrants · Frailty: Bound by the Code of Strength
The Bone-Kings are the self-proclaimed rulers of the wastelands, bound by an unshakable warrior code: only the strongest can lead. Their fortress-cities rise from the corpses of conquered nations. Loyalty is earned in battle — to rise among their ranks, one must challenge and slay those above them. They seek absolute dominion over Xylandra through war and bloodshed.
Goal: Rule all of Xylandra through conquest  |  Nemesis: Skyborne Freeholds
THE SKYBORNE FREEHOLDS
Concept: Free Traders & Rebels · Frailty: No Unified Command
A loose coalition of skyship captains, rebels, relic-hunters, and traders who resist warlord control and favour independence above all else. They have no unified empire — only shared principles. They trade in information, salvage, and passage across the skies, and they will fight fiercely to keep the air free.
Goal: Preserve freedom from warlord rule  |  Nemesis: Bone-Kings & Ironfang Guild
THE RIFT SCHOLARS
Concept: Forbidden Knowledge Seekers · Frailty: Obsession Overrides Morality
A secretive order of scientists, mystics, and rogue academics who study the unstable forces that fracture Xylandra's reality. They believe that by mastering the anomalies, one can reshape the world — perhaps even reverse the disasters that shattered it. Many have disappeared into the Riftlands, emerging years later as something else entirely. They walk a fine line between knowledge and madness.
Goal: Master the Rift anomalies  |  Nemesis: Cult of the Builder-Gods
THE CULT OF THE BUILDER-GODS
Concept: Fanatical Zealots · Frailty: Blind Faith
The Cult worships the Builders as gods and their technology as sacred. They restore and guard Builder ruins, treating relics not as tools but as divine objects. Trespassers at their sites are dealt with harshly. They despise both the Rift Scholars (heretics who meddle with sacred forces) and the Skyborne Freeholds (scavengers who strip holy sites). A Cyber Templar exists in uncomfortable relation to this faction — incorporating Builder tech into the body is either the ultimate devotion or a profound blasphemy.
Goal: Preserve and worship Builder technology  |  Nemesis: Rift Scholars
THE IRONFANG MERCENARY GUILD
Concept: Elite Warband for Hire · Frailty: Loyalty Only to Coin
Feared and respected across Xylandra. Loyal to no king, no cause — only the highest bidder. Hardened veterans of a thousand conflicts, trained in advanced tactics and lethal efficiency. They take no contracts they cannot complete, and once a deal is struck, it is absolute. They have been known to slaughter former allies if the contract demands it. To cross the Ironfang Guild is to invite death.
Goal: Become the dominant mercenary force on Xylandra  |  Nemesis: Skyborne Freeholds
THE GHOSTS OF THE FRACTURE
Concept: Riftborn Outcasts · Frailty: Unstuck from Time
Those who have been so thoroughly exposed to the Rift that they no longer belong to any single moment. They drift between times, sometimes appearing in places before they arrive, sometimes vanishing mid-conversation. Feared by most, pitied by others. They know things they shouldn't — about events that haven't happened yet, about people who haven't been born. They exist outside the normal political landscape because they exist outside normal time.
Goal: Survive being unstuck from time  |  Allied with: Rift Scholars (cautiously)
Xylandra's political landscape shifts constantly. A single battle, lost relic, or betrayal can overturn decades of alliance. Use this as a baseline — your story may alter these dynamics entirely.

RELATIONSHIP KEY
Hostile — Open war or severe conflict. Fighting is common, negotiation only under duress.
Tense — Strong distrust, occasional cooperation when necessary. Could tip either way.
Cold — General dislike, not outright hostile. Competing interests, opposing ideologies.
Neutral — No major conflict or alliance. Interact cautiously, without significant friction.
Cordial — Generally positive, not a formal alliance. Trade, shared interests, occasional cooperation.
Friendly — Strong cooperation, regularly work together.
THE SHATTERED EXPANSE
A vast, broken land of floating ruins, gravity distortions, and ancient Builder relics. Some say the Expanse was once the seat of a great empire, now left to wander time itself. Sections of the landscape phase in and out of existence. Entire lost civilizations have appeared overnight and vanished by morning.
THE WARLORD'S WASTES
A land of crimson dunes, fortress-cities, and eternal conflict. Bone-Kings clash for dominance over the remnants of forgotten technology buried beneath the sand. The heat is lethal. The politics are worse. Everything here is earned through blood.
THE BIOLUMINESCENT JUNGLES
Ancient forests where the plant life itself glows, pulsing with energy that may be natural, Builder-engineered, or something in between. Predators use the light to hunt. Ruins of old civilizations are almost entirely reclaimed by growth. Navigation is nearly impossible without a guide — and guides rarely return from the deep jungle.
THE RIFTLANDS
Territories where temporal anomalies are so dense that time itself is unreliable. Entering the Riftlands means accepting that when you emerge, an unknown amount of time may have passed — or that you may emerge before you left. The Rift Scholars maintain outposts here. The Ghosts of the Fracture call it home. Everyone else avoids it if they can.
THE SKYWAYS & FLOATING RUINS
Fragments of a shattered moon drift in unstable orbits above Xylandra, along with sections of ancient cities that were somehow flung skyward during the Fracturing. The Skyborne Freeholds navigate these aerial territories, treating floating ruins as waypoints, temporary settlements, and salvage opportunities. Skyship combat here is complicated by shifting gravity and unpredictable debris fields.
THE CRYSTALLINE SPIRES
Towers of natural or Builder-grown crystal that rise from the desert floor, some tall enough to reach the lower cloud layer. They resonate with energy — approaching one causes electronics to behave strangely and relics to activate without warning. The Builder Cult considers the Spires sacred. Others use them as navigation landmarks. No one sleeps near them willingly.
THE WARLORD CLANS
Brutal conquerors who rule through strength, blood, and ancient law. Their warriors wield tech-infused weapons and relic-forged armor. Status is displayed through scarification, ritual tattoos, and embedded metal sigils branded into skin. A warlord's heraldry is as much armour as their plate — to be marked by a clan means something everywhere on Xylandra.
THE SKYBOUND NOMADS
Wandering airship traders, relic-hunters, and mercenaries who thrive in the floating ruins and storm-ravaged skyways. Some have never touched the ground in their lives. Their culture is built around the ship — the vessel is home, family, and identity. Skyborne Bards sing epic ballads of fallen cities, using vocal resonance and crystal amplifiers to carry their voices across vast distances.
THE SANDBORN TRIBES
Hardy survivors of the desert wastes, masters of beast-taming, scavenging, and surviving in the deadliest landscapes on Xylandra. They know routes through the sands that don't appear on any map. They've been navigating the wastes since before the Warlord Age and have outlasted every empire that tried to conscript or destroy them.
THE RIFTBORN
Those who have touched the unstable energies of Xylandra, gaining unnatural abilities, cursed knowledge, or fragmented memories of past lives. Some can glimpse moments that haven't happened yet. Some exist in two times simultaneously. Most are shunned. A few are sought out desperately — for what they know, or what they can do.
THE MACHINE-KIN
Descendants of cybernetic warlords, biomechanical sages, and forgotten experiments. Some seek a new purpose; others cling to their lost creators' teachings. The line between biological and mechanical has blurred across generations. A Cyber Templar who incorporates Builder tech as devotion belongs to this cultural lineage, whether they claim it or not.
THE SILENT SCHOLARS
Reclusive historians, monks, and mystics dedicated to unravelling Xylandra's fragmented past. They do not align with the Rift Scholars — their interest is preservation and understanding, not manipulation. They maintain hidden archives of oral history, recovered texts, and pre-Fracturing records. Finding one willing to share what they know is difficult. Finding one at all is harder.
ART & CULTURE
Theater of the Rift — ritual storytelling where actors step into shifting time anomalies to re-enact historical battles in real time. The audience never knows if what they're watching is performance or genuine temporal bleed.

Artifact Crafters — highly valued across all cultures, able to reassemble lost technology into functional weapons, armor, and tools of war. A skilled crafter can name their price anywhere on Xylandra.
Magic in Xylandra is not fully understood. Some believe it is a science too complex for the mortal mind. Others see it as a force woven into the fabric of the world. The distinction between technology and sorcery has been meaningless since the Builders disappeared.
BUILDER-TECH SORCERY
The use of ancient artifacts and long-lost sciences. Capable of altering matter, creating shields, forging energy weapons, or doing things that have no name in current languages. Most practitioners don't understand what they're doing — they've learned to activate devices without understanding the principles behind them. True mastery is almost unheard of. The Cult of the Builder-Gods considers casual use of Builder tech to be desecration.
RIFT-TOUCHED ABILITIES
Those who survive a rift anomaly sometimes develop unpredictable powers. Teleportation. Temporal sight — glimpsing past or future moments. The ability to exist briefly outside of normal time. These powers are not controllable and not consistent. A Rift-Touched person may go months without any manifestation, then experience something world-altering in a moment of stress. The Ghosts of the Fracture represent the extreme end of this — people so thoroughly changed that they no longer function within normal causality.
WARLORD BLOOD RITES
Ancient combat rituals practiced by the Warlord Clans, passed down through generations. Not magic in the Builder-tech sense — something older, something that predates the Builders' arrival on Xylandra. Warriors who complete the rites are said to become harder to kill, not through any measurable biological change, but through something the Rift Scholars have no framework for. The Cult ignores it. The Scholars want to study it. The warriors don't care either way.
USING MAGIC IN PLAY
Magic is handled through the oracle like everything else. Ask whether the relic activates, whether the Rift ability manifests, whether the blood rite holds. Advantage if your character has relevant skills or traits. Disadvantage if your frailty is relevant. Yes, and… means it works and something unexpected happens. No, and… means it fails and something gets worse. The drama is in the uncertainty.

THE LAST SKY-TITAN
Extinct God-Beast
Controls the weather. Flight without limit. Cannot regenerate — every wound is permanent and there will be no more of its kind. Believes itself to be the last guardian of the world. It may be right. Cannot be killed without consequence.
THE HUNGER UNLEASHED
Reality-Devouring Anomaly
Dissolves matter. Walks through time. Exists only to spread. Weak to ancient seals — the Builders apparently anticipated something like this. The Ghosts of the Fracture fight to contain it. This is the one thing most factions on Xylandra quietly agree is a shared problem.
