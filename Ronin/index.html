Aphantasia Sense Anchors

Non-visual grounding for the world of Ronin. These are not descriptions — they are felt presences. Let each sensation arrive without trying to picture it.

⚖ Weight of Steel
The drag and mass of a katana in the hand — not light, never light. The edge of the tsuka wrapped in ray skin: rough, almost sandy against the palm. A slow draw from the saya: a sound like a breath held and released. The balance point sits just past the guard, pulling forward, always wanting to swing. Between draws: the weight pressing against the hip, a constant reminder of what you carry.
🌧 Road Beneath Your Feet
Packed earth that softens in rain and hardens in frost. Grit between the wooden slats of waraji sandals. Uphill roads: the burn that starts in the calves and climbs. Flat roads after a fight: each step lands heavier than the last. The sudden cold of stone underfoot when entering a town. The specific ache of three days' walking — not sharp, but everywhere, a deep settled tiredness.
🌫 Temperature & Air
The cold that arrives before rain — a drop in the air that presses against the face and neck. Morning mist: damp, clinging, slightly heavier than ordinary air. The wave of heat from a fire in a roadside inn — sudden, almost aggressive after hours in the cold. A windstorm carries grit and cedar smell. Snow falls lightly but lands on the back of the neck with a small, precise shock.
👃 Smell of the Journey
Woodsmoke from a village carried long before the village appears — cedar, pine, something damp. Wet hemp robes after rain: heavy, earthy, faintly sour. Sweat and iron after a fight. The dry, papery smell inside a Buddhist temple. A farm: animal warmth, hay, the particular sweetness of fermenting rice. A body of water before you hear it — cold and mineral and alive.
🩹 The Body in Combat
The moment before a draw: stillness, breath held, the whole body listening. The percussion of a blocked strike travelling up both arms. The wrong angle of a missed swing: a yank through the shoulder. A wound: first cold, then burning heat that spreads. The pull of a healing cut — tight, insistent, a reminder on every step. Defeat: the ground arriving without warning.
👘 Texture of Cloth & World
Undyed hemp hakama — rough, stiff when new, worn soft over months. The tight wrap of a obi pressing against the belly. A wakizashi pressing its flat weight against the thigh through fabric. Temple steps: cold, gritty, wide enough to force slow steps. An inn's thin futon: compressed, smelling of other people's sleeps. The grip of a worn tsuka: familiar as your own pulse.
🎵 Sound & Silence
Crows before a fight — three sharp calls, then silence. Rain on a thatched roof: dense, layered, never quite steady. The particular resonance of wooden sandals on a large wooden bridge — hollow, announcing your arrival. A Buddhist monk's bell at dusk. The hiss of steel leaving the saya. Wind through bamboo: a dry, crackling sigh. Silence in a forest that had been singing insects: that sudden absence.
🌙 Pressure of the World
A town that does not want you: the pressure of eyes on the back of the neck. A city with too many people: sound and warmth pressing in from every direction. The relief of open road after walls. Reputation growing: something in how people step aside, heavier and more unsettling than welcome. The weight of your own past — not a thought, but a settled pressure in the chest, present even in sleep.

Play Log

What Is Ronin?

Ronin is a solo narrative game. You play a wanderer — a disgraced samurai, a beggar with a sword, a wave-man with no master — moving from town to town through a fictional feudal Japan. The world unfolds through dice and tables. You stitch the fragments into a story.

You need: this companion, two six-sided dice of different colours (one dark/black, one light/white), and a willingness to improvise.

The core loop: Roll Route → Roll Location → interact with what you find. Repeat. Villains appear as your Reputation grows. Defeat the third villain — the Final Villain — to end the game.

The Dice: Yin & Yang

The dark die (Yin) represents the world, your opponent, or resistance. The light die (Yang) represents you.

Most rolls compare these two dice. You add bonuses to the Yang (light) die. If your final Yang total is greater than the Yin total, you succeed. Draws mean nothing happens.

Extra Effort: At any point, spend 1 Determination to reroll one die. Repeat as many times as you have Determination points.

Attributes

  • Reputation (starts 0, max 6): How well-known you are. Triggers villain appearances. Add to Intimidate rolls. Gained by passing through cities, heroic deeds, sparing enemies.
  • Compassion (starts 2, max 6): Sensitivity and empathy. Add to Charm rolls. Lost by killing or letting innocents suffer. Gained by making allies or helping people. Affects your final Honor.
  • Determination (starts 2, max 6): Willpower. Add to Talk rolls. Spend to reroll dice. Gained by resting in safe places.

Talk: Success reveals Past Information. After a first success, you may try again (+2 to dark die) to reveal a Dark Secret.

Charm: Success converts a Possible Ally into an Ally. Cannot charm while they're in danger.

Intimidate: Success = opponent leaves. Failure = opponent gains +1 to their next Fight roll.

Enemy with Dark Secret revealed: You may also attempt to Talk to an Enemy if you've previously discovered their Dark Secret.

Fight

Both sides roll and add Fight modifiers. Highest wins. Draws = nothing happens (attacks cancelled). Losing side may spend a Block point to negate the attack.

  • If you win: Choose to knock out (+1 Reputation) or kill (−1 Compassion).
  • If you lose and have no Block: Roll a single die. 1 = dead. 2+ = wake up Wounded (−1 Fight), Determination reset to 0.
  • Block points reset at the end of each scene (each Route or Location result).
  • Surrender: Auto-lose. If the enemy spares you, you don't lose Determination.
  • Multiple opponents: Face them one by one, weakest first.
  • Fighter ally vs Villain: Fighter ally goes first. If the ally wins, they leave. If the ally dies, you lose 1 Determination, but the villain cannot recover spent Block.
Innocent ally killed by villain: When you meet a villain, roll 1d6. On 1–2 an Innocent Ally is killed — you lose 2 Determination.

The Journey Cycle

After creating your character, the game is this cycle repeated:

  • 1. Roll Route (Table 11, 2 dice): Determines what happens on the road. May trigger a Villain encounter (check Reputation) or a Road Encounter (Table 13).
  • 2. Roll Location (Table 12, 2 dice): Determines where you arrive. Cities give Reputation. Some locations give Determination or Compassion. Cities and towns may trigger an Urban Encounter (Table 14).
  • Repeat until you've found and defeated the Final Villain.

Allies & Their Roles

  • Mentor: Teaches you their technique. You can choose between your technique and theirs in any fight.
  • Blacksmith: Forges a Special Weapon (+1 Fight) delivered at the next city you reach.
  • Healer: As long as they're alive and your ally, you cannot die when defeated in combat.
  • Fighter: Fights Villains before you. Wins = satisfied, goes their own way. Dies = dead.
  • Innocent: Gives +1 Fight at all times. Risk of being killed by Villains (roll 1–2).

Villains

There are exactly three Villains. Build each with Name (Table 1), Appearance (Table 2), Technique (Table 3). Villains 1 & 2 get a trait from Table 18. The Final Villain uses Table 19 (identity), plus a Scar (Table 6) and Unique Power (Table 20).

Villains appear based on your Reputation when certain Route results come up. The Final Villain's identity may be discovered gradually through mercenaries who speak of their master.

After the Final Villain is defeated: Calculate Honor = (Determination × 2) + Compassion. Check the Honor table on the character sheet. If honor ≤ 2, you may attempt Seppuku: roll 2d6 and add to honor.

Stitching the Story

Ronin gives you fragments — a scar, a nightmare, a family background, a scar's meaning. Your job is to connect them. Ask yourself: Who gave this scar? Why does this nightmare recur? What happened to the family?

Leave loose ends. A character you encountered in passing might become important later. A question left unanswered may find its answer at the worst possible moment. This is the game working as intended.

If your character dies, you may continue by taking an ally (or past character) and restarting with the same villains who have already appeared. The story doesn't end — it inherits.

Glossary

Daimiô — Japanese feudal lord; a samurai who ruled a region.
Iaijutsu — Technique of drawing the sword from its sheath.
Jitte — Pair of metal weapons for disarming and breaking blades.
Kama — Sickle weapon.
Kanabo — Heavy metal bat studded with metal tips.
Katana — Common Japanese long sword.
Kenjutsu — Broad term for sword-fighting technique.
Kusarigama — Kama fused with a long chain and iron weight.
Naginata — Japanese pole weapon.
Niten Ichi-ryu — Technique using a sword in each hand.
Odachi — Very long Japanese sword; blade up to 1 metre.
Oni — Demon; evil spirit.
Ronin — "Wave-man." A samurai who lost his master and honour. Lowest social class.
Seppuku — Japanese ritual suicide; used to restore family honour.
Tonfa — Pair of wooden weapons worn over the forearms.
Tsujigiri — Killing random passers-by to test a new weapon or technique.
Wakizashi — Japanese short sword.
Yokai — Spirit from Japanese folklore.
